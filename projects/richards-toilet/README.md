Case: Madison police had a warrant to search Steiney Richards's motel room for drugs; the judge had struck out the no-knock permission. An officer knocked pretending to be from the hotel; Richards opened the door, saw a uniform and slammed it. Police broke in; he tried to run (out the window) and was caught; cocaine and cash were in the bathroom. Wisconsin's Supreme Court said police never need to knock in a felony drug case. The Supreme Court (9 to 0) threw out that blanket rule, but upheld this entry because slamming the door gave police reason to fear the drugs would be destroyed.
