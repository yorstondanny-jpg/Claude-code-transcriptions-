# Reel prep: Dolan v. United States Postal Service (A/B trial)

Nothing is rendered here. Times are from `audio/words.json` (argument, `audio/argument.mp3`) and `opinion_words.json` (opinion announcement, `audio/opinion.mp3`), in m:ss.ss. Every clip starts and ends on a full sentence and stops before the next speaker comes in, unless the beat is a deliberate exchange. "+laugh" means the clip runs on through the room's laugh, which is measured in the README.

**Title card question:** Can you sue if a delivery left on your doorstep trips you up?

**Sendable ending:** Trip over a parcel the post left on your porch? You're allowed to take the Post Office to court.
From the holding (Kennedy, 7-1): "We disagree and hold that Dolan's suit may proceed." 546 U.S. 481, 483 (2006). It's a right to sue, not a win.

## Checks before cutting
- **Clip 6 speaker:** the official transcript labels "Well, then I'll probably --" and "Then I'll probably be subject to some intentional torts, myself." as MS. MILLETT. But Roberts is talking to Radmore ("you'll be responsible, if you prevail"), during his rebuttal, and the voice on "intentional torts, myself" measures 147 Hz median. That's Radmore's range (141 Hz in his opening), not Millett's (208 Hz). It's very likely Radmore. `words.json` keeps the official label: check by ear and caption it as Radmore if it's his voice.
- **Brand on tape:** "UPS" is spoken just after clip 5 (42:09 to 42:18). It's left out of the cut. If it's added back, draw a generic brown box, no logo.
- **The injury:** never described on tape or in the opinion ("claiming injuries"). Show a trip, nothing more.
- **Audio source:** the argument and the opinion announcement are from Oyez (the Court's recordings, via the National Archives). supremecourt.gov has no audio for this term.
