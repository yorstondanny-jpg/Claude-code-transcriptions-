## Look

**Proportions.** Heads are about 1:4.5 to 1:5 of body height in wide shots (real adults are about 1:7.5). Measured on 0:11 and 0:24: head about 70 to 85 px against 330 to 435 px total height at 960 px frame width. Bodies are short and stocky with thick legs and big boots. Close-ups (0:05, 1:18, end card) use close to real proportions; the caricature is in the face: jaw, hairline, eyebrows, beard shape.

**Line.** Solid black outlines, near uniform weight with slight tapering at the ends. Measured at 1080p: about 8 to 10 px on close-ups, 2 to 4 px on full-body wide shots. Lines are not scaled with the camera, so wide shots look finer. Inner detail lines (wrinkles, shirt folds) are thinner, about half weight.

**Colour.** Flat fills with one shadow tone (a darker tint of the base colour, hard edged). No gradients on characters except a soft gradient on City shirts. Sampled values:
- Pitch: two stripes, #55d05c and #6de368, white lines #fbfffa
- Stands behind pitch: salmon pink #f47a79 with a repeating darker "seat" arc pattern (not crowds)
- Final chorus background: sky blue #90f0fc to #b0e0f7, same arc pattern
- Liverpool shirt #c30c3c with white scribble pattern, sponsor "scouse notenglish"
- City shirt off-white #ddfeff fading to pale blue, sponsor "SHAM"
- Ref kit #252525; skin tones flat, e.g. #fad698
- End card: purple-blue gradient
- Slow-motion and flashback beats: whole frame desaturated grey-green (0:36-0:42, 0:54-1:03)

**Backgrounds.** Almost every shot is the same set: striped pitch, an LED ad board, a flat pink stand. Ad boards are parody brands that double as jokes: "Isaks", "EkitikTok", "Black & Becker", "Coca-Iraola", "Dominiko's Pizza", "bradidas", "Virgil" (Virgin logo). Goal shots add a net and posts. Two one-off backgrounds: the night stadium exterior (1:38-1:41) and the plain blue end card. Backgrounds are cheap to reuse; the camera reframes rather than redraws.

**Mouths and faces.** Replacement mouths, not animated lips. At 10 fps on the end card (1:52) I counted about 5 shapes: closed line, clenched teeth, open oval with tongue, wide smile with top teeth, small "o". Shapes change every 2 to 3 frames at 30 fps (so animated on 2s and 3s). The head stays locked; only the mouth, eyebrows and an occasional blink or wink change. Singing shots (1:44) add looping arm actions (claw hands for "mauled") while mouths swap. Dialogue lines are short so lip sync is loose and nobody notices.

**Motion.** Mostly limited animation: held poses with one moving part (arm raising a card, a banana waving, a head shake). Big actions use smears or speed lines (0:09 whip blur). Falls are a few key poses. Bananas are a running prop: waved overhead by City players and the ref, thrown on the pitch.

### Formulas that repeat across videos (from the captions)

- "The REAL highlights" format (test video, City 2-4 PSG): each real incident or goal gets a gag, then a short chorus refrain comes back after it. In the PSG video the refrain is "That's why we're champions", sung after each goal; in the test video it's "Born in Manchester" after each refereeing decision. The PSG one also has a running commentator joke (his wife and her personal trainer) that pays off in the closing line.
- "The song" format (Barcola): a full parody of a famous song that keeps the original's lyric skeleton. Barcola is "Lola" by the Kinks ("Well I'm not dumb but I can't understand..." and "Well he's not the world's most physical guy..." are near-verbatim Lola lines), with "You'll never walk alone" dropped in as a tag. A spoken comedy middle section ("I'm out of here! Saudi Pro League here I come") sits between choruses, then a chorus reprise, a spoken sting and the outro.
- "I'm out of here!" is a stock line (test video 0:08 and 0:30, Barcola 0:55).
- The outro is always the same template, spoken to camera: "Hit like if you [think / would like] ... Go on, hit like!"
- The PSG video's creator captions aren't available; the Barcola video has creator-written en-GB captions, which give the exact lyric text and use "-" to mark a change of speaker. Worth asking the client whether they have scripts in that form.
